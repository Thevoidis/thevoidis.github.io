Ok I realized something. Meaning is one of things like God that doesn't make sense. It's just one of those things that philosophers made up to complicate life as is.
I remember that back when I was a kid, I used to think about multiverses and shit and write stupid hero stories before Marvel even introduced the multiverse and stff in their movies. Back then, I was never overwhelmed with "The meaninglessness of the universe/multiverse" or anything. I was rather quite excited about so many things, about all the possibilities and all that. I realized that while playing Palworld, I didn't care about there being a "meaning" in the game. I just played the game. Every where I went, I could see a place just further off, and the game just said "You don't need to, but you can go there", and that's all I needed. Same with the tech tree and everything. There were things that I *could* do, but didn't need to, and that was enough for me to do it. And that's all I cared about as a child.

Same thing with God. When I was in high school, I was obsessed with various things about "God", about it's existence, about various aspects of it's existence, the implications of it's existence, etc, and I used to do silly things like read sacred texts of various religions and even do things like follow a certain religion until I was convinced that it was bullshit (I tried following "pagan"/"multiple dieties" Hinduism, Islam , Christianity, and all that). Eventually I came to realize that God was just one of those things that humans came up with just to fry their brains because they had nothing better to do. I didn't care about God when I was a kid, and probably neither did ancient humans who had better things to do.

There was also a point where I was studying quantum mechanics and I was told that "nobody can understand quantum mechanics" and I always kept wondering "Is my understanding wrong ? it's got to be, right ? I must be one of those idiots who thinks they understand but don't really understand anything deeper". But ultimately, I just came to the conclusion that it didn't matter either way. After all, it was just a theory. Not a fundamental thing that you *should* understand. If you were alive hundreds of years ago, would you stress that you "didn't properly understand ptolemy's mechanics of planetary orbits ?" There was probably something deep in it , but it's bound to be replaced by something else at something at some point anyways. It's like a programmer in the 1970s being obsessed with "understanding COBOL at a deeper level" No such thing exists. After all, it is just a language like everything else. Everything in it just comes down to assembly, which comes down to the logic of the transistors , and all that. The quirks of each language are interesting, but you don't need to worry excessively about "quirks".

Yes, "quirks". I find myself worrying excesssively about "quirks" and "deeper understanding" of things that were made just to worry about their "deeper understanding", like "God" and "meaning". I've already been wasting my life atleast thrice worrying excessively about these things that were made from day one with the sole purpose of making people overthink. Why would anyone worry about the "deeper meaning" of something that is completely made up ? Like I remember a quote from Frankl that goes "Those who have a 'why' to live can deal with almost any 'how'". Now, I guess that what he meant was that people need a "meaning" or an "anchor" for them to continue dealing with life. I think that is false. Like I said , people play video games all that time, even if the game's own lore and story have no "meaning", like say, minecraft. All you need is something to do, and you will do it, simply because you have something to do. And if that "something to do" is "worrying about the nature of abstract things", then you shall do it as well. All you need to do is decide what you need to do. You don't actually need a "why" or a "deeper reason". I am convinced that the "Look for a meaning" thing is just propoganda. Because I have observed that people who have never heard or bothered about "Looking dor a meaning" live happily, while a lot of people who "Look for a meaning to live" , just keep "Looking for a meaning to live", and even if they find one, they are devastated when it leaves, not because they lost something precious, but because they have lost their "ideological anchor" that they "looked for".

In short, naturally, humans don't need anything of that. If it is needed, it will come naturally and you won't even know it. There is no point fussing over it.
